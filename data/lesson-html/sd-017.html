sd-017 · Observability

Start with a question the system must answer

Telemetry is useful when it supports a decision about customer behavior and system state.

The green dashboard and the failing assistant

An enterprise assistant reports healthy hosts, low average CPU, and successful HTTP responses. Customers nevertheless wait a minute for the first useful token, and some tool actions complete twice after retries. The monitoring is not necessarily wrong; it measures the wrong boundary. The engineering problem is to infer enough internal behavior from available evidence to explain a customer symptom, identify a safe intervention, and verify recovery. Collecting more data without defining those questions can make the problem more expensive rather than easier.

Monitoring evaluates known conditions, such as an error threshold or missing heartbeat. Observability is the broader ability to investigate system behavior using its outputs and context. Neither word guarantees a product feature. Ask whether an on-call engineer can follow one request across queues, replicas, model execution, and external effects; distinguish delayed from lost work; and identify the deployed configuration involved. A dashboard of aggregate success can coexist with a severe failure affecting one tenant or model.

Define a customer-visible operation before instruments. For streaming inference, headers returned is not the same as first useful token or successful stream completion. For recovery orchestration, plan accepted is not the same as stack recovered safely. For retrieval, an index write is not the same as a fresh authorized query result. These distinctions connect your OCI/FSDR background to AI infrastructure without implying any unprovided personal accomplishment or private OCI implementation.

Telemetry must distinguish desired state, observed state, and authoritative business effects. A log saying “sent request” does not prove the provider completed an action. A metric counting retries does not identify which logical operation repeated. A trace can reveal timing relationships while still lacking the provider's final outcome. Good observability makes these limits visible instead of treating every event as truth.

Use complementary evidence

Metrics summarize populations and trends. Logs record selected events with fields. Traces connect causally related work and timing across components. Profiles describe where execution spends resources. Changes, deployments, policy versions, and topology events provide context. OpenTelemetry documents these signal categories and propagation concepts, but adopting an instrumentation library does not automatically create good boundaries or reliable interpretation.

A practical incident workflow starts with the symptom and its scope, compares healthy and affected populations, follows representative requests, tests a hypothesis against authoritative state, and checks whether intervention improves the customer signal. Correlation can suggest a cause, but simultaneous deployment and latency increase do not prove causality. Use rollback or controlled comparison when safe, and retain alternative hypotheses until evidence distinguishes them.

How I would explain this in an interview: “I design telemetry backward from operational questions. Metrics tell me where impact is changing, traces expose causal paths, logs add specific state, and authoritative records settle business outcomes. I measure the customer boundary first and use infrastructure signals to explain it.” Pause and name three questions a recovery operator should answer without access to raw customer data.

Chapter summary

Observability is a decision capability supported by evidence. Choose meaningful boundaries, join complementary signals, and keep observation distinct from authoritative completion.

02 · Measurement contracts

Define an SLI that cannot hide failure

Numerator, denominator, eligibility, and observation point determine the meaning of success.

SLI, SLO, and agreement

A service-level indicator is a measurement of a defined property. A service-level objective is a target for that indicator over a specified window. A contractual agreement may add business consequences and exclusions. Do not use these interchangeably. A team can target a stricter internal objective than its external contract, and a metric with no explicit target can still be diagnostically useful.

A request-based availability SLI can be good eligible operations divided by all eligible operations. Define eligibility before looking at results. If every overload rejection is excluded because the request never executed, the indicator can improve while customers receive less service. Some legitimate client errors may be excluded, but the rule needs stable semantics and abuse resistance. Separate service-caused rejection from malformed or unauthorized input, and document whether the contract includes capacity shortages.

Observation point matters. A server may count success after writing a response that the client never receives. A gateway may time out while the backend later commits an effect. Client probes and server counters see different portions of reality. Use several observations where needed and state which one governs the objective. Missing telemetry is not automatically a successful request, and double-counted retries can distort both demand and apparent success.

Worked example 1: eligible request accounting

Initial state: a service observes 1,000,000 attempts in a window. Of these, 20,000 are confirmed malformed or unauthorized under a predetermined exclusion rule. There are 5,000 service errors, 3,000 service-capacity rejections, and 2,000 requests that exceed the latency condition. Assume these bad categories are disjoint for the example. The objective is to calculate a good-operation ratio without hiding overload or counting a failure twice.

Eligible denominator is 980,000. Bad eligible operations total 10,000, so good eligible operations are 970,000. The ratio is 970,000 divided by 980,000, approximately 98.9796 percent. A dashboard that reports 995,000 non-error responses out of a million can conceal overload and latency failure, while a dashboard excluding capacity rejection can conceal a different part of the symptom. The contract, not the most flattering formula, chooses the calculation.

Intermediate instrumentation should identify a stable logical operation and its final classification. If one attempt is both a server error and slow, the bad set should normally count that operation once for a combined good-event SLI. Keep component counters separately for diagnosis. Failure variation: retries create three attempts for one user action. Decide whether the objective measures attempts or logical actions, then use consistent identities rather than mixing a logical numerator with an attempt denominator.

Final outcome is an auditable SLI with explicit exclusions and category overlap rules. Production implication: publish the measurement specification alongside the dashboard, version changes, and test edge cases. Interview takeaway: the numerator is easy; the denominator and observation boundary determine whether the objective honestly represents service.

Several objectives may be necessary

Availability, latency, freshness, durability, and correctness can fail independently. A fast stale response may satisfy a latency target but violate freshness. A successful tool response with a duplicated business effect may violate correctness. Avoid compressing all quality into one opaque score. Use a small set tied to meaningful capabilities and make operational tradeoffs explicit. A degraded mode counts as good only if the product contract actually permits it.

Chapter summary

An SLI is a versioned measurement contract. Define eligible work, good behavior, overlap, identity, and observer before setting a target or interpreting an incident.

03 · Error budgets and alerting

Page on meaningful budget consumption

A burn rate connects observed failures to the objective's allowed error fraction.

Derive burn rate

If the objective is success fraction S, the allowed error fraction is one minus S. Burn rate is observed bad fraction divided by allowed bad fraction, using compatible definitions. A burn rate of one spends budget at the average pace implied by the objective under the current traffic assumptions. A burn rate of ten spends it ten times as fast. The ratio is dimensionless; a forecast about exhaustion additionally needs the remaining budget, traffic behavior, and window model.

Google's SRE workbook provides public guidance on alerting using budget burn and multiple time windows. The numerical scenarios here are original applications rather than copied thresholds. A short window reacts quickly but can be noisy; a longer window provides evidence of persistence but can delay detection. Combining windows can require both an acute symptom and sustained impact. Choose thresholds from desired detection, budget spend, and operator action rather than copying an arbitrary rule.

Low traffic complicates fraction-based alerts. One failed operation out of two yields fifty percent error but little absolute evidence. A small set of critical operations may still justify immediate action. Combine count, coverage, synthetic probes, and capability importance deliberately. Do not suppress all low-volume failure as noise if that low-volume path is the disaster-recovery control needed during an outage.

Worked example 2: a sustained two-percent failure rate

Initial state: a request-based objective targets 99.9 percent good eligible operations over thirty days. For a simple constant-traffic illustration, the allowed bad fraction is 0.001. The service begins failing two percent of eligible work. The objective is to quantify burn and choose an alert response. Burn rate is 0.02 divided by 0.001, or twenty. If that rate and traffic pattern persisted from a fresh budget, the thirty-day allowance would be consumed in about 1.5 days.

Thirty days contain 43,200 minutes. One hour at twenty-times budget pace consumes 20 times 60 divided by 43,200, or about 2.7778 percent of a full period's budget under these assumptions. Five minutes consumes about 0.2315 percent. A brief burst and an hour-long incident have different budget consequences even at the same observed error fraction. For varying traffic, use actual eligible event counts rather than interpreting elapsed time alone as spend.

Intermediate state includes the measured error numerator, eligible denominator, window, and objective revision. Failure variation: a query returns no samples because the telemetry path failed. Treating absent data as zero errors creates a silent green dashboard. Monitor data completeness and independent probes, and distinguish no traffic from missing observations. Another variation is a rolling window with prior incidents; the remaining budget differs from a fresh-period estimate.

Final outcome is a page or ticket tied to meaningful impact and an actionable owner. Production implication: attach runbook, affected capability, scope, deployment context, and evidence window to the alert. Interview takeaway: derive the ratio and budget spend, then explain which assumptions permit a time-to-exhaustion forecast.

Make alerts actionable

A page should indicate a condition requiring timely human action, not every interesting metric change. Group correlated symptoms without losing affected tenant or regional scope. Define routing, escalation, deduplication, and recovery notification. Test alerts using recorded or synthetic incidents and confirm that the query matches the SLI. An alert that never reaches the right operator is a failure of the reliability system, even if its expression is mathematically correct.

Chapter summary

Burn rate relates observed bad work to allowed error, while exhaustion depends on actual budget and traffic. Multi-window evidence, missing-data handling, and operational routing make the math useful.

04 · Metrics and distributions

Aggregate populations, not precomputed percentiles

Metric type, labels, and histogram boundaries determine which questions can be answered.

Counters, gauges, and latency histograms

A counter accumulates events and can reset when its process restarts; a rate calculation must handle those resets under the metric system's semantics. A gauge describes a current quantity such as queue depth or active sequences. Histograms accumulate observations into distributions, enabling threshold counts and approximate quantiles when the representation supports aggregation. A summary exposing a precomputed local quantile generally cannot be combined by averaging those quantiles into a global percentile.

Choose buckets or a native-histogram configuration around meaningful decisions. If the latency objective is under 300 milliseconds, an observation boundary near that threshold can support the good-event count more directly than a coarse distribution with a huge gap. Unit consistency matters: seconds and milliseconds mixed under the same metric name produce nonsense while still rendering a graph. Version semantic changes rather than silently changing the meaning of one series.

Prometheus documentation grounds histogram and summary behavior; the examples here use simplified exact counts to isolate reasoning. A production estimator has bucket approximation, scrape or export loss, aggregation windows, and sampling considerations. Do not label an interpolated quantile as a precise observed value without explaining its uncertainty.

Worked example 3: averaging p99 hides a slow population

Initial state: worker A serves 9,900 requests all at ten milliseconds. Worker B serves 100 requests all at one second. The objective is the global tail, not an average worker score. Averaging the workers' local p99 values gives 505 milliseconds if weighted equally, but that number is not a valid global p99. Weighting local percentiles by request count is also not a general solution, because percentiles do not add linearly.

The exact global sample has 9,900 observations at ten milliseconds and 100 at one second. Under a nearest-rank definition, rank ceil(0.99 times 10,000) is 9,900, so p99 is ten milliseconds; p99.9 falls in the one-second group. Other interpolation conventions can differ near the boundary. This example deliberately exposes the definition, and also shows why one tail number can miss a small severe population. An SLI threshold and per-population views may be more actionable.

If B instead serves 200 one-second requests while A still serves 9,900, the slow fraction exceeds one percent and the nearest-rank p99 lies in the slow group. Aggregate bucket counts or raw compatible distributions before estimating, and retain useful dimensions such as region and model. Intermediate state should include observation count, not only the percentile. Failure variation: a sampling policy drops slow records disproportionately, biasing the reconstructed distribution.

Final outcome is a defensible distribution query with a known convention. Production implication: measure threshold compliance directly where possible and inspect tails by meaningful population. Interview takeaway: averaging local p99 values produces neither an exact global p99 nor a reliable bound, and even a correct percentile does not substitute for impact scope.

Cardinality is a capacity decision

A time-series identity is the combination of metric name and labels. Tenant, model, region, status, worker, request ID, and path can multiply into an enormous series population. Use bounded dimensions for metrics and move high-cardinality investigation fields into an appropriate event or trace system. Do not put raw prompt text, user secrets, or arbitrary URLs into labels. A cardinality cap can drop data, so observe that drop and document which questions remain answerable.

Chapter summary

Metric semantics determine valid aggregation. Preserve counts and meaningful distributions, control cardinality, and avoid treating one global percentile as a complete view of customer impact.

05 · Traces and causal context

Follow the work, including asynchronous boundaries

A trace is a causal model, not a sum of all span durations.

Context propagation and span meaning

A trace groups related work, while spans describe selected operations and their parent or link relationships. Propagate context through authenticated request metadata and durable messages where appropriate. An asynchronous batch may combine several causal inputs, so a simple parent tree can misrepresent its origin; links or explicit correlation records can preserve those relationships. Do not assume that a trace ID is a business idempotency key or an authorization credential.

Choose span boundaries that explain waiting, execution, and dependency time. A single span covering queue wait plus GPU execution can reveal total delay but not where it arose. Separate admission, queueing, model preparation, prefill, decode, retrieval, tool dispatch, and response delivery when that detail supports decisions. Excessive tiny spans can create cost and noise, so instrument the meaningful control boundaries rather than every function call.

Cross-host clocks can be skewed, making naive wall-time subtraction misleading. Local monotonic durations and causal relationships often provide stronger evidence than apparent timestamp order. A child appearing to start before its parent may indicate clock or instrumentation problems, not time travel or necessarily a business-order violation. Record uncertainty and avoid using telemetry clocks as a substitute for the authority protocol.

Worked example 4: parallel branches and the critical path

Initial state: a request spends twenty milliseconds in a gateway, then runs retrieval for eighty milliseconds and a policy lookup for thirty in parallel. After both finish, model work takes 200 milliseconds and delivery takes ten. Ignore overlap outside the stated branches. The objective is to explain end-to-end time. The serial total is 20 + max(80,30) + 200 + 10, or 310 milliseconds. Adding every span gives 340 and double-counts the overlapping thirty-millisecond branch.

The critical path goes through retrieval rather than policy in this trace. Improving policy from thirty to ten does not shorten the modeled end-to-end time, while improving retrieval from eighty to forty reduces it to 270 milliseconds. That conclusion is local to this execution and its assumptions. If policy sometimes takes 150 milliseconds, it can become critical in a different population. Preserve distributions and dependency context rather than optimizing one representative trace as if it described all traffic.

Intermediate trace state includes operation identities, causal joins, queue or wait spans, and deployment/model version. Failure variation: a tool succeeds but the response is lost, so the trace ends with a timeout. The trace supports diagnosis but does not prove the tool failed. Query authoritative effect state using stable business identity. Another variation is missing propagation through a queue; link the durable message identity to the consumer span without treating them as unrelated transactions.

Final outcome is a causal explanation of latency with a known missing-evidence boundary. Production implication: retain exemplars connecting metric anomalies to representative traces and annotate changes. Interview takeaway: optimize the measured critical path, distinguish overlap from serial work, and use authoritative records for effect correctness.

Bound context and protect trust

Untrusted callers can inject enormous baggage or forged identifiers. Bound metadata size, validate formats, and do not treat propagated tenant fields as authenticated merely because they arrived in a trace header. Baggage can reach third-party services, so exclude secrets and sensitive prompt content. Trace access itself needs authorization because topology, resource IDs, and error details may be confidential even without raw payloads.

Chapter summary

Traces explain causal paths and overlap, not just elapsed numbers. Propagate bounded trusted context, represent asynchronous relationships, and separate diagnostic evidence from business authority.

06 · Structured events and sampling

Keep enough evidence without making logs the outage

Sampling, schema, privacy, and durability change what an investigation can conclude.

Design structured diagnostic records

A useful event has a stable schema, timestamp semantics, service and deployment identity, operation class, outcome, reason, and correlation references. Free-form prose is valuable for a human explanation but difficult to aggregate reliably if every component names the same condition differently. Preserve original error detail where allowed while normalizing a bounded error category. Avoid logging a success message before the state transition it claims has committed.

Diagnostic logs and audit records have different contracts. Losing a sampled debug event may be acceptable; losing evidence of a privileged policy change may not be. Route durable audit through an appropriate authoritative path, with access, retention, and tamper considerations. Do not make every application request synchronously depend on a remote diagnostic sink unless that is an explicit business requirement. Observability should not become the easiest way to disable the data plane.

Sample to control cost, but state the selection rule. Uniform random samples can support estimates with uncertainty. Error-biased or tail-based samples improve diagnosis but do not represent the original traffic proportions without a valid weighting method. Sampling after a component crashes may miss precisely the most important failures. Preserve aggregate unsampled outcome counters where appropriate and independently observe telemetry loss.

Worked example 5: an error-biased trace sample

Initial state: one million requests include 1,000 errors and 999,000 successes. The true error fraction is 0.1 percent. A policy retains every error trace and one percent of successful traces. Expected retained successes are 9,990, giving approximately 10,990 retained traces in total. The objective is to diagnose failures without misreading the sampled population as the availability denominator.

The retained sample's naive error ratio is 1,000 divided by 10,990, about 9.099 percent, far above the true 0.1 percent. That is expected selection bias, not necessarily a service incident. If the inclusion probabilities and sampling assumptions are valid, weighted estimates can recover population counts: each sampled success represents about one hundred successes, while each error represents one. For an operational SLI, unsampled aggregate counters can be simpler and less error-prone.

Intermediate sampling metadata should identify the policy and, where used, its inclusion probability. Failure variation: tail sampling keeps slow traces only after buffering, but collector memory pressure drops some before the decision. The apparent tail is now biased by capacity loss. Measure ingestion, predecision drops, completed decisions, and export success separately. Another variation is a rare critical path with too few events; deterministic retention for that path may be justified without pretending its distribution matches overall traffic.

Final outcome is a diagnostic sample whose limits are explicit. Production implication: do not calculate an objective from an investigation view unless its population and weighting support that use. Interview takeaway: a sample can be excellent for finding a failure and unsuitable for estimating how often the failure happens.

Privacy and cost are architectural constraints

Raw prompts, tool arguments, access tokens, and retrieved documents can expose sensitive information. Prefer redacted metadata and approved diagnostic capture with explicit scope and lifetime. Hashing a low-entropy identifier may not anonymize it. Apply tenant access controls, deletion policy, and data residency to telemetry stores and exports. A centralized log search can become a cross-tenant data bypass unless query authorization is designed as carefully as production access.

Chapter summary

Structured events and sampling support investigation only under a known evidence contract. Separate diagnostic loss tolerance from durable audit, and make privacy and telemetry drops visible.

07 · Pipeline capacity and reliability

Observe the observability system

Collectors, buffers, indexes, and queries have their own failure and isolation boundaries.

Collection and aggregation architecture

Instrumentation can export to a local agent or shared collector, which validates, batches, samples, and routes data to storage. Local buffering absorbs brief disruption but needs byte and age limits. A durable queue can decouple ingestion from indexing when required, while adding retention and replay responsibilities. Choose which signals may drop, aggregate, or block rather than treating every telemetry byte as equally valuable.

Separate ingest and query budgets. An expensive ad hoc search should not prevent new incident evidence from arriving. Tenant quotas, cardinality limits, schema validation, and query timeouts protect shared systems. A collector configuration rollout is production code: malformed processors or routing rules can discard all signals. Canary changes, retain a known-good config, and validate completeness with independent heartbeat or synthetic events.

Meta's historical Scuba paper grounds low-latency exploratory analysis of high-volume events. It is not evidence that its exact architecture is appropriate for every modern observability workload. The design here uses general principles: bounded ingestion, explicit sampling, dimensional queries, and resource isolation. Current retention and performance numbers are hypothetical unless attributed.

Worked example 6: series and event volume

Initial state: 200 services each expose a metric across ten regions, four outcome classes, and fifty model labels. If every combination exists, that single metric family can create 200 times 10 times 4 times 50, or 400,000 series before instance and other labels. Adding a request ID with one thousand distinct values per combination can create an enormous further multiplier. The objective is to choose bounded dimensions before memory and query cost explode.

For an independent event-volume estimate, assume 50,000 spans per second averaging 600 serialized bytes. That is 30 MB per second decimal, or 2.592 TB per day before replication, indexing, compression, and reserve. A ten-percent uniform sample reduces the nominal serialized retained volume to 259.2 GB per day, but it also changes investigative coverage. These are different calculations: series cardinality affects metric state, while event bytes affect trace/log ingestion and storage.

Intermediate state includes active series, churn, accepted bytes, dropped bytes, queue age, and index delay. Failure variation: an incident raises span size and error retention simultaneously, multiplying volume precisely when operators need the system. Reserve critical ingest, cap payloads, and shed lower-value telemetry under an explicit policy. A fixed sample percentage does not guarantee fixed bytes if record size changes.

Final outcome is a capacity plan with declared completeness and degradation. Production implication: benchmark ingestion and investigation together under incident-shaped load, not only steady traffic. Interview takeaway: the observability platform must survive the failures it helps diagnose, and its own omissions must not look like service health.

Recovery and independent evidence

Use independent probes or a separate minimal path to detect collector or storage failure. A telemetry pipeline cannot reliably prove its own completeness using only events that traverse the failed component. During recovery, replay can overload indexes or skew apparent event time; distinguish occurrence time, ingestion time, and query visibility. Retain lineage or source identity so duplicate replay does not inflate counters or audit conclusions.

Chapter summary

Budget cardinality, bytes, churn, and query work independently. The telemetry platform needs isolation, recovery, and a way to reveal its own incomplete observations.

08 · AI and recovery observability

Measure useful tokens, safe effects, and real recovery

Hardware health and HTTP success explain only part of an AI service.

Inference and training signals

Separate time to first token, inter-token or per-output-token latency under a defined calculation, total completion latency, prompt and output lengths, queue time, and failure classification. A short answer and a long generation have different expected duration. Report conditional populations by model and workload class without unbounded labels. A low mean latency can result from rejecting expensive work, so interpret latency together with admission and completion.

NVIDIA DCGM Exporter provides public GPU telemetry context. Device utilization, memory, power, errors, and fabric indicators can explain symptoms, but high utilization alone does not prove useful inference throughput or training progress. A GPU can be busy recomputing failed work. A synchronized training job can be blocked by one rank while other devices appear differently utilized. Connect device evidence to job attempt, placement, and accepted checkpoints.

For training, track useful steps, checkpoint age and validity, restart time, lost accelerator-time, and stragglers. The objective is progress toward an accepted model under cost and deadline constraints, not merely a running process. Distinguish application numerical instability from infrastructure faults so automatic restart does not loop forever on deterministic bad input. Keep low-level profiling scoped because detailed capture can perturb timing and consume resources.

Agents and external effects

An agent trace should connect the logical run, model calls, planning revisions, approvals, tool identities, and durable outcomes. Do not log hidden or sensitive reasoning by default to compensate for missing state design. Persist the execution artifacts the product needs, and instrument their transitions. Anthropic's public multi-agent account establishes the importance of coordination and observability in that workload; it does not reveal a private trace schema or storage protocol.

For effectful tools, show requested, dispatched, accepted by provider when known, completed, failed, and unknown outcomes separately. A timeout span can coexist with a successful external action. A dashboard counting tool retries should expose stable logical action identity for authorized investigation without leaking raw arguments. The accepted effect ledger, not trace decoration, answers whether a destructive action already occurred.

For a hypothetical OCI recovery platform, measure impact onset, detection, authority/fencing, state restoration, dependency recovery, validation, and routing. RTO ends at the agreed business capability, and RPO uses a complete recovery frontier. Record plan version, epoch, selected frontier, and evidence references. A fast accepted plan is not a successful recovery, and an unobserved step should remain uncertain rather than be colored green.

Company context and transferable judgment

OpenAI's PostgreSQL publication supplies public examples of diagnosing query and workload pressure. Google's SRE guidance grounds symptom-based monitoring and objectives. Meta's Scuba research grounds exploratory dimensional analysis. NVIDIA documents accelerator telemetry, while Anthropic provides agent-system context. These sources establish relevant public problems; the lesson's SLO values, capacity estimates, and proposed AI observability architecture are original hypothetical designs.

How I would explain this in an interview: “I join customer objectives to execution and resource evidence. Token latency tells me the inference symptom, spans locate waiting and work, GPU signals explain resource behavior, and durable state resolves business effects. I keep each signal's limits explicit and make telemetry failure observable.”

Chapter summary

AI observability connects useful completion and safe effects to model, workflow, and hardware evidence. Different signals explain different boundaries; none alone proves the whole service recovered.

09 · Production design

Build an AI observability platform with explicit completeness

A hypothetical architecture connecting objectives, telemetry, investigation, and audited action.

Requirements and data model

Design a platform for 200 services, multiple inference models, long-running agent workflows, and training pools. Assume 50,000 spans per second before incident bursts, bounded high-value metrics, and a need to identify affected tenants without exposing their content. Customer objectives cover eligible request success, latency thresholds, retrieval freshness, and safe recovery completion. Diagnostic signals may be sampled; privileged changes require durable audit. These are proposed requirements, not private company numbers.

Instrumentation produces versioned metrics and structured events with service, deployment, region, workload class, outcome, trace context, and approved resource references. Local agents batch and bound export. Regional collectors authenticate producers, validate schema, enforce payload/cardinality budgets, redact, sample, and route. A durable ingest path is used for selected signals where its cost is justified. Metric storage, trace/event storage, audit, query execution, and alert evaluation have separate quotas and availability contracts.

Expose ingest with tenant/service identity and schema version; query with authorized scope, time range, dimensions, and resource budget; objective definition with numerator, denominator, exclusions, window, owner, and revision; and incident evidence links. Store occurrence and ingestion time separately. An alert instance records query revision and evidence window so a later rule change does not silently rewrite what the operator saw. Audit action authorization separately from diagnostic query results.

Define retention by purpose. Short detailed traces can support debugging, longer aggregate metrics support trends, and audit follows its own requirements. Data residency and deletion apply to indexes, replicas, exports, and backups. A reference to sensitive data may still be sensitive. Attribute cost by accepted bytes, series, query work, and retained storage so teams can improve instrumentation without losing essential evidence.

Normal incident flow and failure behavior

A customer-boundary burn alert identifies affected operation class and window. The operator compares regions, models, and deployment cohorts, follows exemplars to traces, checks queue and dependency signals, and consults authoritative state for uncertain effects. A change timeline offers a hypothesis, not automatic causation. A scoped mitigation such as reducing concurrency, disabling a bad rollout, or isolating a tenant is authorized through the production control plane, not executed merely because a dashboard link was clicked.

If a collector overloads, it preserves critical counters/audit according to policy and sheds lower-value diagnostics with explicit drop metrics. If storage query capacity fails, ingestion can continue within bounded buffering. If ingest fails, independent probes report evidence loss and the UI distinguishes stale data from healthy service. Replayed events retain source identity and event time so incident charts do not present old failures as new or double-count them without notice.

At 30 MB per second serialized spans, a fifteen-minute buffer needs about 27 GB before replication and overhead. That capacity is finite; a longer storage outage requires deliberate loss, alternate retention, or producer backpressure that must not collapse the application. Do not let every instrumented service block indefinitely on export. Critical audit that cannot be lost may instead require an application-specific durable acceptance contract and a different failure response.

Scaling and architecture defense

Partition ingestion by authorized service or tenant scope, but avoid hot partitions from one huge trace or model. Separate index construction from raw retention where useful, and bound query fan-out. Precompute stable SLI counts while retaining drill-down evidence under sampling policy. Query authorization must apply before returning aggregates that could expose another tenant. A sophisticated UI cannot repair an ingest schema that omitted the dimensions needed to isolate impact.

Runbooks cover cardinality explosions, missing-data alerts, collector rollback, lost audit acceptance, query overload, delayed replay, and privacy incidents. Canary schema changes and collectors using synthetic sentinel events with known expected paths. Test alert delivery and on-call ownership as part of the system. The operational goal is shorter safe diagnosis and recovery, not maximum telemetry volume.

Define investigation access as a time-bounded operation with a recorded purpose. An engineer may need a tenant-specific trace during an incident, but that need does not imply permanent access to every prompt or tool argument. A diagnostic session can grant a narrow resource scope, require approval for sensitive capture, and expire automatically. Record who requested the session, which fields were exposed, and whether exports were produced. Test revocation and deletion of derived investigation artifacts as well as primary records. This makes emergency visibility useful without creating an informal second authorization system that survives after the incident ends.

Compare a managed backend, an integrated self-hosted platform, and separate signal stores. Managed storage reduces infrastructure work but does not define SLIs, access policy, cardinality, or correct sampling. Separate stores allow specialized economics but require correlation and operational ownership. Defend a design using measured investigation needs, cost, completeness, and team capacity, and record the workload change that would force a new choice.

Chapter summary

The platform needs explicit data completeness, authorized correlation, independent failure detection, and isolated ingest/query/alert paths. Telemetry is valuable when it improves safe operational decisions.

10 · Executable measurement checks

Test the denominator and burn math

Small deterministic models make measurement assumptions reviewable.

What the code establishes

The Python example calculates a good-event ratio, burn rate, period budget consumed under constant traffic, and a nearest-rank percentile from exact samples. It deliberately names the percentile convention. It also demonstrates the biased trace-sample ratio and event-volume arithmetic. These are standard-library educational calculations, not queries against a live telemetry backend.

The input checks reject impossible event counts and invalid objective fractions. A production SLI must additionally define overlap, exclusions, retries, observation point, missing data, and window boundaries. A mathematically correct function cannot fix a collector that counts successful headers as complete streaming responses. The code is useful because reviewers can test the formula separately from instrumentation and then validate the mapping between them.

from math import ceil

def burn(bad, total, target):
    if total <= 0 or not 0 <= bad <= total or not 0 < target < 1:
        raise ValueError('invalid measurement')
    return (bad / total) / (1 - target)

def nearest_rank(values, q):
    if not values or not 0 < q <= 1:
        raise ValueError('invalid quantile')
    return sorted(values)[ceil(q * len(values)) - 1]

Inject one slow population, an error-biased sample, a lost propagation header, clock skew, and a collector restart. 11 · Original interview practice

Defend the evidence behind the dashboard

Original preparation questions, not confirmed interviews from any company.

Twelve Senior Staff challenges

1. The dashboard is green but customers cannot finish requests. Where do you start?

Evaluation: boundary selection. Define the failed capability and observer, then compare its good-event SLI with component health. Headers, process liveness, and accepted jobs may precede useful completion. Follow-up: only one model is affected. Preserve meaningful cohort dimensions. Mistake: adding telemetry before defining the question. Tradeoff: detailed cohorts improve diagnosis but require controlled cardinality and enough evidence.

2. Should overload rejections be excluded from availability?

Evaluation: honest contract. Decide eligibility before observing outcomes; service-caused capacity rejection may be bad eligible work under the promised capability. Distinguish abuse and malformed input without hiding service failure. Follow-up: retries multiply attempts. Choose logical or attempt identity consistently. Mistake: optimizing the denominator after an incident. Tradeoff: a precise policy is more work but makes budget and customer reporting defensible.

3. What does burn rate twenty mean for a 99.9-percent objective?

Evaluation: quantitative assumptions. Observed bad fraction is twenty times 0.001, or two percent. Exhaustion time additionally depends on remaining budget and traffic/window behavior. Follow-up: one hour in a thirty-day constant-traffic period. About 2.7778 percent of full budget. Mistake: presenting a forecast as a universal guarantee. Tradeoff: short windows detect faster but need persistence and evidence rules.

4. Can you average per-host p99 values?

Evaluation: aggregation. No general rule makes averaged quantiles a global percentile. Aggregate compatible distributions/counts or use threshold compliance. State estimator and population. Follow-up: one percent is very slow. A correct p99 can still hide a severe minority. Mistake: weighting local p99 by traffic as a fix. Tradeoff: richer distributions cost storage but support meaningful aggregation.

5. Why does a trace's total duration differ from summed spans?

Evaluation: causal timing. Parallel spans overlap; use joins and the critical path, not a simple sum. Clock skew and missing propagation add uncertainty. Follow-up: an asynchronous queue breaks the parent tree. Use appropriate links and durable correlation. Mistake: optimizing a noncritical branch. Tradeoff: more boundaries explain time but increase instrumentation cost.

6. The trace timed out. Did the cloud action fail?

Evaluation: evidence versus authority. Not necessarily. The provider may have accepted or completed it. Query the durable effect ledger/provider using stable business identity. Follow-up: retries appear in several traces. Correlate logical action separately. Mistake: treating diagnostic status as business truth. Tradeoff: authoritative reconciliation can take time but prevents unsafe duplicate effects.

7. Why does retaining every error trace inflate the apparent error fraction?

Evaluation: selection bias. Sampling probabilities differ between successes and errors. Use unsampled counters or a justified weighted estimator, not naive sample ratios. Follow-up: collector drops traces before tail sampling. Observe missingness. Mistake: computing the SLI from a diagnostic view. Tradeoff: biased retention improves investigation while reducing simple population inference.

8. How do you prevent telemetry cardinality from becoming an outage?

Evaluation: capacity and schema. Bound metric labels, cap descriptor growth, use events/traces for appropriate high-cardinality investigation, and expose drops/churn. Follow-up: a new model label is unbounded. Validate schema at ingest. Mistake: putting request IDs in every series. Tradeoff: removing dimensions saves cost but may limit incident isolation; choose deliberately.

9. Should telemetry failure stop inference?

Evaluation: signal contracts. Diagnostic export can often degrade with bounded loss and independent detection. Required audit may need a durable acceptance path and different behavior. Follow-up: all buffers fill. Apply the declared policy rather than blocking accidentally. Mistake: one universal answer. Tradeoff: evidence completeness, data-plane availability, and cost must be explicitly negotiated.

10. What does GPU utilization tell you about training progress?

Evaluation: useful work. It is resource evidence, not proof of accepted progress. Join job attempts, step rate, checkpoint validity, stragglers, and restart loss. Follow-up: GPUs repeatedly recompute failed work. Track useful accelerator-time. Mistake: equating busy with productive. Tradeoff: detailed profiling can perturb workload and needs scope/budgets.

11. How do you observe the telemetry system itself?

Evaluation: independent evidence. Measure ingest/export drops, queue age, freshness, and query health, with probes not depending exclusively on the same failed path. Follow-up: no samples could mean no traffic. Distinguish coverage states. Mistake: interpreting absence as zero failures. Tradeoff: independent paths add infrastructure but prevent silent blindness.

12. How do you make logs safe for enterprise AI?

Evaluation: privacy and access. Minimize payloads, redact secrets, scope authorized diagnostic capture, enforce tenant query boundaries, residency, retention, and deletion. Hashing alone is not anonymization. Follow-up: traces leave for a third-party tool. Validate propagated metadata and export policy. Mistake: treating telemetry as harmless infrastructure data. Tradeoff: detailed evidence helps diagnosis but creates governance and security exposure.

Architecture defense: blind during a regional incident

The interviewer removes the regional collector while inference latency rises and asks whether to declare recovery. Clarify independent customer probes, last observation time, affected scope, audit availability, and authoritative workflow state. A strong response marks telemetry incomplete, uses bounded independent evidence, and avoids converting missing metrics to success. It preserves safe running work, restores collection under an explicit priority, and verifies customer capability before closing the incident. The model answer accepts uncertainty rather than fabricating precision from stale charts.

Defend every measurement with its population, selection rule, authority, and failure behavior. Operational clarity matters more than a large dashboard inventory.

12 · Speaking and recall

Explain the measurement before the graph

Practice a precise argument about evidence, objectives, and operational action.

Thirty-second explanation

Observability helps us explain customer behavior using evidence. I define good eligible operations and their observation boundary first, then combine metrics, traces, structured events, and authoritative state. Error budgets turn objectives into operational priorities. Sampling, missing data, and cardinality affect interpretation. The telemetry platform needs its own reliability, access controls, and cost limits.

Two-minute explanation

I would start with what the customer must be able to do. For streaming inference, headers are not completion; for recovery, accepted intent is not a restored stack. I would define the indicator's numerator, denominator, exclusions, identity, and measurement point, then choose an objective and window.

Metrics reveal population change and threshold compliance. Traces locate causal paths, including queues and parallel branches. Structured events provide specific state and error context. Authoritative records settle whether a business effect happened. I would not use a timeout span as proof that a provider action failed.

For alerting, burn rate compares observed bad fraction with allowed error. Multiple evidence windows can distinguish a transient spike from sustained budget loss. Missing telemetry needs explicit treatment and independent probes. For latency, I would aggregate compatible distributions rather than average local percentiles, and retain enough dimensions to identify affected models or regions without unbounded labels.

Finally, I would budget ingest, retention, and query separately, make sampling bias and drops visible, and protect prompts and tenant data. I would test alert delivery and investigation under incident-shaped load. The objective is a shorter safe path from symptom to explanation and recovery, not maximum telemetry volume.

Five-minute explanation

Suppose we are instrumenting a global AI platform. I would begin with customer capabilities and failure observations, not a list of tools. Inference availability, token latency, retrieval freshness, safe tool execution, and recovery completion each have different good-event conditions. I would specify those conditions and the eligible population before setting numerical targets.

That measurement contract would include retries and exclusions. If capacity rejection is a service failure under the product promise, excluding it would hide impact. If an event is both slow and erroneous, a combined bad-operation indicator should avoid double-counting while component metrics remain available. I would version semantic changes so historical budget reports stay interpretable.

I would choose complementary signals. Bounded metrics provide rates and distributions, traces connect admission, queueing, model work, retrieval, and tools, and structured events capture important transitions. A change timeline helps form hypotheses. None of these replaces the effect ledger for a cloud action whose response was lost. The system should show unknown outcomes rather than invent success or failure.

Latency requires careful aggregation. Parallel spans overlap, so the critical path is not their sum. Local p99 values do not combine by averaging. A correct global percentile can still hide a severe minority, so I would pair threshold compliance with meaningful cohort views. Clock skew, lost propagation, and sampling would remain visible limitations rather than disappear behind a polished chart.

For objectives, I would derive burn rate as observed bad fraction divided by allowed bad fraction. A two-percent error rate against a 99.9-percent target burns at twenty times the nominal pace. Forecasting exhaustion requires traffic and remaining-window assumptions. I would choose alert windows and routing based on action urgency, evidence, and budget consequences, then replay representative incidents through the actual evaluator.

The telemetry platform has its own architecture. Agents and collectors need byte and CPU budgets, validation, redaction, and known loss behavior. Ingest, query, and alert evaluation should not starve one another. An incident can increase error retention and event size, so I would test that correlated surge. Independent probes must reveal when the collection path is blind. Required audit follows a different durability contract from sampled debug data.

For AI specifically, I would connect useful token completion and training progress to hardware evidence. Busy GPUs may be recomputing failed work; a fast response may be unauthorized or stale. Agent traces need durable run, plan, approval, and action references without indiscriminate sensitive capture. The final design is defensible when operators can identify impact, test a hypothesis, authorize a safe intervention, and verify real customer recovery under explicit evidence limits.

Important concepts and final cheat sheet

  • SLI: defined measurement.
  • SLO: target over a window.
  • Eligibility: denominator contract.
  • Error budget: allowed bad work.
  • Burn rate: observed versus allowed error fraction.
  • Histogram: aggregatable distribution representation under compatible semantics.
  • Cardinality: distinct metric identities.
  • Critical path: causal latency boundary.
  • Sampling bias: selection affecting population inference.
  • Completeness: known coverage and loss.
  • Audit: required evidence with its own contract.
  • Exemplar: link from a metric observation to detailed evidence.
  • Useful progress: accepted outcome, not resource activity.

Avoid equating no samples with no errors, averaging p99 values, using sampled traces as an unsampled denominator, and treating logs as authoritative effects. Replace those shortcuts with the measurement specification and a failure trace. Useful phrases include “this sample is diagnostic, not representative,” and “this observation cannot establish the provider outcome.”
