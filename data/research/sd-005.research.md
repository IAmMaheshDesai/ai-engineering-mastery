## Core protocols and migration evidence

1. Giuseppe DeCandia and colleagues, *Dynamo: Amazon's Highly Available Key-value Store*, SOSP 2007. Relevant sections cover partitioning, replication, virtual nodes, membership, hinted handoff and reconciliation. Chapters 2 and 4 use Dynamo as a historical example of design tradeoffs for availability, placement and version reconciliation. The lesson's majority-replicated release ledger is a separate hypothetical system. https://cdn.amazon.science/ac/1d/eb50c4064c538c8ac440ce6a1d91/dynamo-amazons-highly-available-key-value-store.pdf

2. Fay Chang and colleagues, *Bigtable: A Distributed Storage System for Structured Data*, OSDI 2006. Its ordered row-key model and tablet organization ground chapter 3's locality discussion and chapter 4's separation of key boundaries from server assignments. https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi-06/bigtable-distributed-storage-system-structured-data

3. Vitess maintainers, *Reshard*, version 18.0 documentation. Primary-traffic switching stops source writes and waits for the target to catch up. Reverse replication supports a subsequent reversal when enabled. Chapters 5 and 8 use the concepts to derive an original generic migration protocol. Product commands, defaults and exact implementation details depend on the deployed version. https://vitess.io/docs/archive/18.0/reference/vreplication/reshard/

## OpenAI

4. OpenAI, *Scaling PostgreSQL to power 800 million ChatGPT users*, 2026. The published account describes read replicas and migration of suitable write-heavy workloads to sharded stores while retaining a primary PostgreSQL deployment for other workloads. This is direct evidence that an architectural choice may differ across workloads within one organization. https://openai.com/index/scaling-postgresql/

5. OpenAI, *ChatGPT Availability Impacted*, February 4, 2026 incident write-up. Removal of a read replica during maintenance interacted with capacity pressure and retries. The account motivates chapter 2's attention to degraded capacity. https://status.openai.com/incidents/01KGMVV926ZSCD1MSDBS07AWYA/write-up

Original interview practice. Estimate headroom after losing a replica, then defend an alternative for a write-heavy workload. The chapter 7 figures and topology are illustrative and do not describe OpenAI's internal registry.

## Meta

7. Meta Engineering, *Open-Sourcing Rebalancer: A Generic, High-Performance Library for Solving Assignment Problems*, September 21, 2026. The account identifies shard-to-server assignment as an application of a general optimization system. Chapter 6's illustrative multi-resource planner is original and does not reproduce Meta's objective function. https://engineering.fb.com/2026/09/21/open-source/rebalancer-generic-high-performance-library-assignment-problems/

Original interview practice. Separate hard placement constraints from move cost, demonstrate an infeasible solution and specify how to avoid making a second move in response to transient load.

## Google

Bigtable (source 2) provides verified historical evidence for distributing sorted row ranges and using tablet boundaries as an operational unit. Original interview practice. Design a key layout for a per-tenant timeline and quantify the cost of merging the newest fifty records across eight stripes. Include the snapshot and tie-breaker contract needed for repeatable pagination.

## NVIDIA

8. NVIDIA, *Disaggregated Serving*, Dynamo documentation, publication date unspecified. The guide explains how prefill and decode workers may scale independently while transferring KV cache state. It motivates distinguishing reconstructible serving state from durable release authority. It does not establish that cache transfers implement database replication. https://docs.nvidia.com/dynamo/latest/knowledge-base/concepts/system-architecture/disaggregated-serving

Original interview practice. Decide which state should move with a serving worker, which state can be safely recomputed and which metadata must survive failures as authoritative history. Compare network transfer cost with recomputation and customer latency.

## Clarifications and traceability

Chapter 1 separates copying a dataset, splitting its logical boundaries and changing placement. Chapter 2 distinguishes receipt, durability, commitment and query application. Chapter 3 makes explicit why a perfect hash cannot distribute one indivisible hot key. Chapter 5 defines a caught-up target using a fenced, complete source frontier. Chapter 8 explains why restoring old routing after a new target write can discard an acknowledged result. These clarify potential errors in design reasoning rather than correcting supplied research.
