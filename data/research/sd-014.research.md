## OpenAI — verified public engineering evidence

The PostgreSQL publication discusses overload and retry amplification; the historical Kubernetes account provides bursty large-workload infrastructure context. These establish why asynchronous pressure and control-plane isolation matter. They do not identify OpenAI's internal broker, partition key, or event-effect protocol.

**Original practice:** a recovery event burst and cache-miss/retry load threaten the same authority service. Separate authoritative work from observations, bound retries, and define the admission response. Defend useful business completion rather than process or queue health. All capacities and proposed mechanisms in the lesson are hypothetical.

## Anthropic — verified public evidence and limits

The 2025 multi-agent article discusses coordination and production reliability in a tool-using application. It supplies a relevant setting for durable observations and long-running work. It does not establish an internal consumer-group or log architecture.

**Original practice:** replay an agent observation stream without resending irreversible tools. Keep business effect identity, transport position, and execution authority separate. Design reconciliation after a lost tool response. These are original preparation exercises, not claims about Anthropic's private implementation.

## Meta — verified historical engineering evidence

The 2017 LogDevice article presents logs as reliable ordered record sequences and discusses data-intensive use cases. It grounds the retained-history abstraction while also illustrating why one must inspect actual record-position semantics rather than assume every log has contiguous numbering.

**Original practice:** choose entity-local ordering, diagnose a hot key, and distinguish append order from application completion order. The four-partition throughput values and routing migration discussion are original analysis. The source does not establish every present Meta log system.

## Google — verified public research evidence

## NVIDIA — verified technical documentation
