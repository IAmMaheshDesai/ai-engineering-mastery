## OpenAI — verified public engineering evidence

The PostgreSQL scaling account discusses overload, retries, and protection across several layers. It grounds the need to protect backend resources rather than count only edge traffic. It does not establish the lesson's bucket parameters, global grant protocol, or private OpenAI admission implementation.

**Original practice:** hold arrivals constant while a dependency slows, and defend concurrency protection plus bounded queues. Explain how cache misses and retries can move the bottleneck. The Staff+ skill is tracing useful resource occupancy and failure amplification, not copying a universal rate threshold.

## Anthropic — verified public product evidence

The rate-limit documentation establishes public API usage constraints, distinct from spend restrictions. It provides context for multi-dimensional customer-facing capacity contracts. The lesson deliberately avoids prescribing current tier values, prices, or private enforcement implementation.

**Original practice:** distinguish language-model tokens, limiter credits, active memory, and durable money. Design accounting for unknown output and durable agent resume. Explain why a public rate allowance does not guarantee immediate execution capacity or safe external effects.

## Meta — verified public engineering evidence

It does not establish that the lesson's global authority or exclusive grants reproduce Meta's implementation.

## Google — verified public operational guidance

The SRE overload chapter provides public grounding for protecting useful service through overload handling and client-side behavior. It does not establish a numeric SLO or a current Google AI-serving limiter design.

**Original practice:** reject work that cannot fit its remaining deadline, coordinate retries across layers, and evaluate adaptive controls with delayed feedback. Explain why a queue and autoscaler cannot instantly create usable capacity.
