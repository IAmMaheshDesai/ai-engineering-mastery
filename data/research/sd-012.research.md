## OpenAI — verified public engineering evidence

The January 2026 PostgreSQL article describes a single primary and geographically distributed read replicas. That directly motivates distinguishing active regional reads from independent writers. It establishes only the deployment described in that publication, not a universal OpenAI topology or any private recovery commitment.

**Original practice:** defend a global read-heavy platform while preserving an ordered write boundary. Explain replica lag, session observations, and why a local frontend can still depend on remote writes. The Senior Staff skill is choosing semantics by operation instead of equating traffic scale with multi-master necessity. No public interview report or company RPO/RTO promise was verified.

## Anthropic — verified public evidence and limits

## Meta — verified historical engineering evidence

The 2013 TAO account describes a primary region per shard and replication to other regions, with eventual consistency as its default setting. It grounds the pattern of globally serving data while retaining partition-level primary authority. It must not be generalized to every current Meta data system.

**Original practice:** design a versioned ownership directory and move one tenant between homes without permitting dual writers. Discuss hot partitions and cross-home transactions. The exact epochs, migration states, event numbers, and proposed API are original analysis.

## Google — verified public engineering evidence

The 2012 Spanner paper describes globally distributed, sharded, synchronously replicated data with Paxos groups and externally consistent transactions. It motivates separating group replication from cross-group transaction semantics. The lesson does not derive Spanner's full protocol from its brief overview or claim that clocks alone establish transaction correctness.

**Original practice:** evaluate a five-voter 2–2–1 placement, enumerate region-loss sets, and locate required remote acknowledgment. The RTTs, capacities, and topology are invented teaching values rather than Google measurements. Defend how a cross-shard transaction adds a separate atomicity boundary.

## Corrections and unresolved questions

No supplied technical research required correction. Misconceptions corrected in the lesson include active ingress versus write authority, convergence versus quota enforcement, routing versus promotion, and quorum intersection versus a complete consistency proof.

Private company region layouts, recovery objectives, current protocol implementations, internal latency, and interview rubrics remain unverified. Historical sources are dated explicitly. The replica-placement arithmetic assumes surviving voters communicate; the counter assumes stable monotonic writers and eventual delivery; the capacity model assumes uniform redistribution and a measured fixed workload ceiling. Real provider fencing, cross-region benchmarks, residency decisions, and failover drills were not executed. The code and browser checks validate teaching artifacts and limited models, not a live production architecture.
