# Distributed transactions, sagas and compensation

Independent research reviewed November 23, 2026. The supplied master prompt defines instructional scope, the supplied HTML template defines presentation, and the keyboard-first application defines catalog identity and supporting resource schemas. The configurations, timelines, providers and capacities in the worked examples are original hypothetical designs. They are not descriptions of any employer's internal recovery system.

## Core engineering evidence

1. Hector Garcia-Molina and Kenneth Salem, *Sagas*, Princeton technical report, January 7, 1987. The authors motivate breaking long-lived work into locally committed transactions with business compensations. Chapters 4–5 distinguish the resulting amendable intermediate state from rollback of an uncommitted database transaction. https://www.cs.princeton.edu/techreports/1987/070.pdf

2. Jim Gray and Leslie Lamport, *Consensus on Transaction Commit*, Microsoft Research, research publication. Supports chapters 2–3 on transaction commitment, failure assumptions and the difference between resource-manager votes and replication of a decision. A replicated coordinator in the lesson is an architectural example, not an implementation of the paper's Paxos Commit protocol. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/consensus-on-transaction-commit/

3. PostgreSQL Global Development Group, *PREPARE TRANSACTION*, current manual, accessed on the review date. Documents persistence beyond a session, recovery through finalization commands, retained locks and operational risks of long-lived prepared transactions. Chapter 3 uses these specific properties. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-prepare-transaction.html

4. Amazon Web Services, *Saga orchestration pattern*, AWS Prescriptive Guidance, publication date unspecified. Supports chapters 4–6 on centralized orchestration, application-level consistency, compensation, idempotency and the cost of recovery. A separate database does not by itself rule out atomic commitment when managers expose compatible prepare interfaces. The lesson tests capability at the participant boundary. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/cloud-design-patterns/saga-orchestration.html

5. Amazon Web Services, *Making retries safe with idempotent APIs*, Amazon Builders' Library. Provides context for client request identity, retries, parameter binding and late arrivals. Chapters 5–7 derive their own transaction and provider examples from these concerns. https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/

## Google

6. Google Cloud, *Life of Spanner Reads & Writes*, multi-split write section, current documentation accessed on the review date. The documented design combines replicated participant state with a coordinator for cross-split commitment. Supports the distinction between replication and participant obligations. Implementation details depend on the stated isolation and locking mode. https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/whitepapers/life-of-reads-and-writes

Original practice: Trace a participant failing after prepare, draw the durable decision and defend a read policy while different splits finish applying. Defend whether a local transaction could remove a distributed dependency from a GPU budget reservation service.

## Meta

7. Meta Engineering, *RAMP-TAO: Layering atomic transactions on Facebook's online graph store*, August 18, 2021. The published work addresses fractured reads in a read-heavy graph service. It supports the value of stating the exact visibility anomaly an application needs to prevent. The source does not claim that a saga automatically supplies this read abstraction. https://engineering.fb.com/2021/08/18/core-infra/ramp-tao/

Original practice: Design a catalog showing model revision and associated serving metadata. Distinguish the write outcome from the reader's ability to observe a coherent multi-object result. Explain what a stale progress display may cause a downstream automated consumer to do.

## OpenAI

8. OpenAI, *Scaling Kubernetes to 7,500 nodes*, January 25, 2021. The account documents a large research cluster and motivates realistic questions about placing training work across constrained capacity. It provides workload context only. No evidence from this article establishes a particular reservation, compensation or billing design. https://openai.com/index/scaling-kubernetes-to-7500-nodes/

Original practice: Combine a budget reservation, GPU allocation and external launch. Clarify what a successful job admission promises and recover a running job whose launch acknowledgment was lost.

## Anthropic

9. Anthropic, *Staff+ Software Engineer, Infrastructure (Distributed Systems)*, publicly listed role, publication date unspecified. ## NVIDIA

10. NVIDIA, *Checkpointing*, Megatron Bridge documentation, current page accessed on the review date. The technical context is saving and loading distributed training checkpoints. The lesson's restore evidence, immutable manifest and control-plane publication protocol are independent design exercises. https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/megatron-bridge/latest/training/checkpointing.html

Original practice: Separate validated checkpoint readiness from permission to launch the destination workload. Identify which staged data can be collected after cancellation without invalidating another job's restore dependency.

## Teaching synthesis and limitations

The eight worked examples, twelve interview questions, executable models and inline diagrams are original. Chapters 1 and 7 frame success at the boundary the customer observes. Chapters 2–3 distinguish durable affirmative votes, a global decision and local visibility. Chapters 4–6 address compensation under concurrent writes, uncertain remote completion and duplicated delivery. Chapter 8 supplies model-based failure schedules and a numerical repair-load exercise.

Claims about specific production systems are limited to their cited public sources. Numeric timings and capacity limits illustrate dependency analysis and are not vendor benchmarks. Exact locking, visibility, timeout behavior, heuristic recovery rules and deduplication horizons depend on participant implementation and contract. No independent public evidence was found establishing that any of the twelve practice questions appeared in an interview at the target firms.
