# Distributed consensus for AI control planes

Reviewed November 22, 2026. The engineering question is which decisions must survive a leadership change, what their ordering costs, and which effects fall outside the replicated log. The eight numbered examples and the design interview are original teaching scenarios.

## OpenAI

**Verified public engineering evidence.** OpenAI described its experience operating Kubernetes at 7,500 nodes, including API server load, etcd, and isolation of event traffic. The account provides a concrete reason to distinguish high-volume observations from authoritative metadata. [Scaling Kubernetes to 7,500 nodes, 2021](https://openai.com/index/scaling-kubernetes-to-7500-nodes/)

**Original practice.** A GPU fleet recovers from an outage and thousands of workers report back at once. Decide which changes need a durable quorum and which can use lossy or asynchronous reporting. Defend queue isolation and admission control using degraded-mode commit latency and time to first safe placement. This tests prioritization and capacity judgment. The published deployment is historical and does not establish current group sizes or interview questions.

## Anthropic

**Verified public engineering evidence.** Anthropic’s engineering guidance distinguishes predefined workflows from agents that choose steps and interact with tools. Tool feedback gives an execution system observable evidence about the environment. [Building effective agents, December 19, 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)

**Original practice.** A tool succeeds while the executor crashes. Explain why committing the workflow intent with Raft still leaves an ambiguous external outcome. Compare provider-side deduplication, lookup by a caller-generated key and a pause for reconciliation. The Staff+ skill is negotiating a precise cross-service contract. The article establishes the tool-use context, not Anthropic’s internal workflow storage protocol.

## Meta

**Verified public engineering evidence.** Meta reports that interruption risk grows as more GPUs participate in a large training job. Its engineering account describes reliability and infrastructure challenges at training scale. [How Meta trains large language models at scale, June 12, 2024](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/06/12/data-infrastructure/training-large-language-models-at-scale-meta/)

**Original practice.** A placement controller fails during a multi-host job. Decide whether to replicate a reservation, a training checkpoint manifest, or all model tensors through the same consensus group. Defend preserving running jobs while temporarily withholding new conflicting placements. A Staff+ answer quantifies wasted accelerator time and separates scheduler recovery from full job recovery.

## Google

**Verified public engineering evidence.** Google’s Spanner paper describes Paxos replication groups and the additional mechanisms supporting distributed transactions and external consistency. [Spanner, OSDI 2012](https://research.google/pubs/spanner-googles-globally-distributed-database-2/)

**Original practice.** A placement must atomically claim GPUs owned by two different metadata groups. Explain what each group’s replicated log establishes and what cross-group transaction coordination adds. Compare a staged reservation protocol with colocating resources likely to be allocated together. Defend the consequences for latency, fragmentation and blocked recovery. Per-group consensus alone orders only that group’s changes.

## NVIDIA

**Verified public engineering evidence.** NeMo documentation describes local checkpoints, optional replication, and recovery using valid checkpoints. [NeMo Framework resiliency features, release 25.07](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/25.07/resiliency.html)

**Original practice.** An old training attempt finishes writing checkpoint pieces after the new attempt starts. Use immutable pieces plus a consensus-backed manifest publication that checks the run incarnation and expected revision. Defend the cost of reconciling orphaned pieces against the throughput cost of routing large tensors through consensus. The checkpointing documentation supplies the workload; the proposed manifest protocol is a design exercise.

## Protocol reading

- Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014. Work through Sections 5–6, especially the prior-term counterexample and joint configuration. Then read Section 8 for client interactions and read-only queries. [In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm, extended version](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/raft-extended.pdf)
- Lamport, 2001. Trace the value-selection rule in Section 2 and the extension to a state machine in Section 3. Keep a separate column for promised ballots and accepted proposals. [Paxos Made Simple](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/paxos-simple.pdf)
- Fischer, Lynch and Paterson, 1985. Focus on the asynchronous model, deterministic protocols and a possible nonterminating execution. The result constrains universal progress guarantees. [Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/tds/papers/Lynch/jacm85.pdf)
- etcd project, release 3.6. Compare the key-value and watch guarantees before designing a control-plane read path. Document which reads consumers can use for admission decisions. [etcd API guarantees](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/learning/api_guarantees/)
