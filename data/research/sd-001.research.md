# Distributed consensus · engineering evidence and interview practice

Independent research, reviewed November 23, 2026. The uploaded prompt defines the curriculum and the uploaded HTML template defines the presentation. Neither supplies empirical evidence. The keyboard-first project provides the catalog, reader and resource schemas. The earlier consensus lesson was replaced by newly authored chapters, examples and questions.

## Protocol foundations

1. Diego Ongaro and John Ousterhout, *In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm*, USENIX ATC 2014, extended version, §§5–8 and Figure 8. Supports chapters 2–4 and 6 on elections, log matching, direct commitment, membership transitions and read safety. The five-voter schedule in chapter 4 is an original numerical adaptation of Figure 8. https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.824/papers/raft-extended.pdf

2. Leslie Lamport, *Paxos Made Simple*, 2001, §§2–3. Supports chapter 5 on phase-one value selection, durable promises, chosen values, learners and the transition to state-machine replication. https://systems.cs.columbia.edu/ds2-class/papers/lamport-paxos.pdf

3. etcd project, *Performance*, version 3.1 documentation. Supports chapter 8's distinction between network and durable storage latency, batching and different read modes. Historical benchmark numbers depend on hardware and version. All latency values in the lesson are independent hypothetical calculations. https://etcd.io/docs/v3.1/op-guide/performance/

## OpenAI

4. OpenAI, *Scaling Kubernetes to 7,500 nodes*, January 25, 2021. The published case describes the control-plane challenges of large Kubernetes clusters serving research workloads. It establishes a real scaling context for reserving scarce control-plane throughput. It does not establish today's cluster size or a private checkpoint implementation. https://openai.com/index/scaling-kubernetes-to-7500-nodes/

Original practice for chapters 7–8. Separate high-volume progress observations from low-volume placement decisions. Show the impact of draining one zone on a quorum and on the restore-ready checkpoint watermark. Defend the different consistency requirements of scheduling and token serving.

5. OpenAI, *Interview Guide*, official recruiting guidance, publication date unspecified. This is evidence about hiring communication, with no claim that OpenAI asks the exercises in this lesson. https://openai.com/interview-guide/

## Anthropic

6. Anthropic, *Staff+ Software Engineer, Infrastructure (Distributed Systems)*, public role description, listing date unspecified. The role situates Staff+ engineering in distributed infrastructure. It supports relevance to the target role, without identifying an internal choice of consensus protocol. https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/4970314008-0

Original practice for chapters 1 and 7. Coordinate ownership changes across training infrastructure and storage teams. Specify the boundary between authorizing a new attempt and enforcing its epoch. Defend the cost of a shared authority against the operational burden of multiple sharded authorities.

## Meta

7. Mahesh Balakrishnan and colleagues, *Virtual Consensus in Delos*, OSDI 2020. The Facebook research team describes a virtual replicated log that allows its underlying implementation to evolve. This gives a documented example of separating a consumer-facing replicated log from mechanisms implementing it. https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/balakrishnan

Original practice for chapters 5–6. Draw the boundary at a log implementation change. Ask what must carry across it for a reader to see a single committed prefix. Defend an incremental migration given mixed versions and incomplete repair.

## Google

8. Tushar Chandra, Robert Griesemer and Joshua Redstone, *Paxos Made Live — An Engineering Perspective*, PODC 2007. The authors describe building production replicated storage for Chubby and problems beyond the core algorithm. https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en/us/archive/paxos_made_live.pdf

9. Mike Burrows, *The Chubby Lock Service for Loosely-Coupled Distributed Systems*, OSDI 2006. This is historical evidence for coarse-grained coordination and the need for protected servers to validate authority. https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/osdi06/tech/full_papers/burrows/burrows_html/

Original practice for chapters 2, 5 and 8. Explain the durable state a replacement coordinator must recover. Design an externally checkable outcome when the elected coordinator can no longer reach the resource it was asked to modify.

## NVIDIA

10. NVIDIA, *Megatron-LM integration*, NVIDIA Resiliency Extension version 0.7.0 documentation. The documented distinction between checkpoint scheduling, finalization and process recovery motivates the publication service exercise. The replicated pointer service in chapter 7 is an original architecture. https://nvidia.github.io/nvidia-resiliency-ext/fault_tolerance/integration/megatron.html

Original practice for chapters 7–8. Quantify pointer traffic and shard traffic separately. Explain what happens if a rank dies during asynchronous checkpoint I/O and defend the evidence required to expose a manifest to a new training attempt.

## Corrections, assumptions and traceability

Chapter 4 explicitly corrects the tempting rule that counting any majority proves any Raft entry committed. Chapter 5 separates acceptance, choice and learning. Chapter 6 separates local leadership from authority to serve a linearizable read. Chapter 7 separates manifest publication from data durability. These clarify common design errors and are not corrections to user-supplied research.

All architecture diagrams, state matrices and sequence drawings are original inline SVG. The voting counterexample attributes its conceptual source above. All traces, capacity assumptions and SLO conflicts are illustrative. OCI recovery scenarios are hypothetical teaching exercises and make no claims about Oracle implementation details or the learner's personal accomplishments. No illustrated capacity, latency or recovery time is a company measurement.
