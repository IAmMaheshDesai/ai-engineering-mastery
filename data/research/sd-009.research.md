# Durable execution, workflow engines and crash recovery

Independent research reviewed November 23, 2026. The supplied master prompt establishes curricular requirements and the template establishes reader behavior. The run journal, provider contracts, numerical assumptions, incident timelines, diagrams, worked examples, interview prompts and speaking answers in this lesson are original teaching designs. They illustrate a proposed architecture and do not describe private OCI or target-company implementations.

## Workflow and provider contracts

1. Temporal, *Understanding Temporal*, documentation. The history, command and activity model informs chapters 1–3. A recorded decision can survive worker loss, subject to the actual service's durability assumptions. https://docs.temporal.io/evaluate/understanding-temporal

2. Microsoft, *Durable Task programming model*, documentation. Decision replay and at-least-once activity execution have different implications. A lost activity completion can cause real activity code to run again. Chapters 1, 3 and 4. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/durable-functions-types-features-overview

3. Microsoft, *Durable orchestrations*, documentation. Re-executing orchestrator logic can reconstruct variables from history and replay results of previously completed activities. Chapter 3. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/durable-task/common/durable-task-orchestrations

4. Temporal, *Activity Definition*, documentation. Activity idempotency, timeout and heartbeat guidance informs chapter 4. The billing, cancellation and provider semantics must still be established for the chosen external service. https://docs.temporal.io/activity-definition

5. Temporal, *Continue-As-New — Go SDK*, documentation. The next run receives the logical current state via parameters. Chapter 8 treats outstanding work and migration as explicit protocol concerns. https://docs.temporal.io/develop/go/workflows/continue-as-new

6. AWS, *Choosing workflow type in Step Functions*, developer guide. Standard and Express workflow execution semantics differ, and explicit Retry changes what can execute again. The language in this guide applies within the product's defined scope. A client's second workflow or a separate provider action requires its own analysis. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/step-functions/latest/dg/choosing-workflow-type.html

7. AWS, *Restarting state machine executions with redrive*, developer guide. The documented redrive behavior retains previous successful step results and execution history, with constraints on eligible executions. This is a real alternative to blindly restarting a whole plan. Chapter 6. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/step-functions/latest/dg/redrive-executions.html

8. Haoran Zhang and colleagues, *Fault-tolerant and transactional stateful serverless workflows*, OSDI 2020. Beldi is a research example with explicit logging, transaction, invocation and garbage-collection mechanisms. Its guarantees depend on its system model; a generic remote provider does not acquire those guarantees simply by being called from a workflow. Chapters 1–2 and 8. https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/zhang-haoran

9. Oracle, *Resume a Disaster Recovery Plan Execution*, Full Stack Disaster Recovery documentation. Public product context for recovery-plan operations. The lesson's journal schema, ownership design, provider adapters and capacity assumptions are hypothetical. https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/iaas/disaster-recovery/cssgm/manage-disaster-recovery-plan-executions/resume-a-disaster-recovery-plan-execution.html

## OpenAI

10. OpenAI, *Scaling Kubernetes to 7,500 nodes*, January 25, 2021. The historical engineering account describes automated detection and removal of unhealthy nodes and monitoring of GPU errors. This establishes a real operational motivation for repair automation. https://openai.com/index/scaling-kubernetes-to-7500-nodes/

11. OpenAI, *Software Engineer, Platform Systems*, public role description, listing date unspecified. Its remit includes distributed systems for visibility and reliability of large training workloads. https://openai.com/careers/software-engineer-platform-systems-san-francisco/

Original interview practice. Design a durable node-replacement operation. Distinguish admission, safe resubmission, observation and application-level readiness. Show which work remains when the orchestration engine itself is fully healthy.

## Anthropic

12. Anthropic, *Effective harnesses for long-running agents*, engineering publication, 2025. The article describes support for incremental progress across context windows. It motivates the lesson's distinction between conversational context and externally verified effects. https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents

13. Anthropic, *Staff+ Software Engineer, Infrastructure (Distributed Systems)*, public role description, listing date unspecified. The role provides context about training, serving and operating infrastructure. https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/4970314008-0

Original interview practice. An agent loses its context after requesting GPU capacity. Specify which tool arguments, permission evidence, business key and provider outcome a successor needs. A natural-language summary alone cannot resolve a remote write.

## Meta

14. Meta Engineering, *How Meta trains large language models at scale*, June 12, 2024. The engineering publication gives context for large-scale training infrastructure and recovery requirements. https://engineering.fb.com/2024/06/12/data-infrastructure/training-large-language-models-at-scale-meta/

Original interview practice. Design a fan-in over immutable checkpoint shards, then distinguish a coordinator's durable history from a manifest that can actually restore a training run. Measure cold recovery time following a worker fleet restart.

## Google

15. Tyler Akidau and colleagues, *MillWheel: Fault-Tolerant Stream Processing at Internet Scale*, VLDB 2013. The paper describes managed persistent state and record processing under an explicitly scoped fault-tolerance model. MillWheel is a stream processor. It supplies a comparison with a long-lived business workflow, not evidence of either system implementing the other. https://research.google/pubs/millwheel-fault-tolerant-stream-processing-at-internet-scale/

Original interview practice. Compare a compact dataflow record transition with a long workflow containing human approvals, provider operations and cancellation. Identify precisely where transactional state updates suffice and where an external contract is required.

## NVIDIA

16. NVIDIA, *Run and follow a training job*, NVIDIA Recovery documentation. The public guidance separates managed job recovery from the workload's responsibility to restore a durable training checkpoint. https://docs.nvidia.com/recovery/running-jobs

Original interview practice. Prove that a replacement attempt loads compatible data after a scheduling failure. Ask whether the optimizer state, shard topology and data cursor are recoverable before equating worker restart with training progress.

## Interview reports, corrections and limitations

17. Anonymous candidate, *Meta | E6 | Onsite | US*, LeetCode Discuss, publication date unverified. The writer reports fault-tolerance follow-ups in an infrastructure design round. This is unverified evidence about one interview format, not corroboration of a durable-execution question at any firm. https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/5559774/Meta-or-E6-or-Onsite-or-US/

The twelve lesson questions and all system-design exercises are original. No verified report establishes their use in a particular interview. Named primary sources substantiate the relevant technical mechanisms and workload context. The lesson corrects common conflations between replay and activity redelivery, provider acceptance and user readiness, activity timeout and provider cancellation, and workflow recovery point and application data recovery point. No provider TTL, capacity, clock bound, SLA or exactly-once external effect is assumed as a fact about a vendor. The lesson's numerical examples explicitly state their hypothetical inputs.
