# SD-007 · Idempotency, retries, deduplication and exactly-once effects

Reviewed November 23, 2026. Independent research. The supplied v8.2 prompt and keyboard-first project establish content and integration requirements. Sources below support specific technical or interview-context claims. The recovery gateway, workload sizes, incident traces, code models and original questions are teaching exercises.

## Technical sources

1. Malcolm Featonby, Amazon Builders’ Library, *Making retries safe with idempotent APIs*. The article explains caller intent, mismatched parameters under reused tokens and late-arriving requests. Chapters 1, 2 and 7 apply these ideas to multistep recovery, canonicalization and retention. https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/

2. Stripe, *Idempotent requests*, API documentation. The documented API illustrates a specific policy for retaining and returning earlier request outcomes. Chapter 3 distinguishes permanent business outcomes from failures before admission. Other providers can choose different policies. https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests

3. Apache Kafka project, *Design*, release 4.1, message delivery semantics. Kafka describes the transactional boundary around input position and Kafka output, the significance of read-committed isolation, and the cooperation required of external destinations. Chapter 5 applies the boundary to a distinct billing ledger. https://kafka.apache.org/41/design/design/

4. AWS, *Well-Architected Framework*, October 2023 edition, retry guidance. The reliability guidance discusses capped retries, exponential backoff and jitter. Chapter 6 derives its own illustrative load and drain-time bounds. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/pdfs/wellarchitected/2023-10-03/framework/wellarchitected-framework-2023-10-03.pdf

## Corrections and interpretation

The lesson explicitly resolves three common errors. A server-generated handle cannot recover itself from a lost first response. Broker-scoped transaction semantics do not make an external side effect transactional. Deleting all evidence of a key removes the ability to distinguish expired intent from a previously unseen key unless another valid record or acceptance rule survives.

All illustrations, sample data, executable models and twelve interview prompts were created for this lesson. Cloud examples are hypothetical and do not describe a private OCI, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google or NVIDIA architecture. A real adapter must confirm the deployed endpoint's key namespace, retention, replay policy, concurrency behavior, failure classification and consistency of lookup results. Publication dates for living API documents and the anonymous report were not inferred.
