## 4. MANDATORY TEACHING METHOD

For every major concept, teach through this progression:

**1. Engineering Problem:** Start with a realistic production scenario, requirements, constraints, and limitations of naive approaches.

**2. Intuition:** Explain the central idea in plain English before introducing formal terminology.

**3. First Principles:** Define assumptions, invariants, guarantees, failure models, and correctness requirements.

**4. Internal Mechanics:** Explain algorithms, states, transitions, protocol messages, data structures, persistence, ordering, concurrency, and recovery.

**5. Worked Example:** Use concrete servers, requests, terms, log indexes, worker IDs, timestamps, or other appropriate values. Show intermediate states.

**6. Failure Injection:** Examine crashes, network partitions, delayed messages, stale ownership, retries, duplicate requests, and partial recovery.

**7. Production Engineering:** Explain scalability, latency, throughput, capacity, observability, reliability, security, operations, and cost.

**8. Alternatives:** Compare plausible designs and explain why one is preferable under specific constraints.

**9. AI Infrastructure Connection:** Relate the concept to LLM serving, GPU scheduling, distributed training, AI agents, or control planes where appropriate.

**10. Interview Explanation:** Provide a natural, technically precise spoken explanation demonstrating Senior Staff-level understanding.

Define unfamiliar terminology before using it extensively.

Do not oversimplify technical guarantees.
